**How much to trust the matcher**

- *Chance matches:* every non-forecast figure was also perturbed by a random 3–25% (10 draws each, 6020 total) and re-classified. **12.3%** of those wrong numbers were still accepted as grounded or derived (dollar amounts 10.9%, percentages 19.8%). That is the matcher's approximate false-positive rate, so the traceable share above is an upper bound by roughly that margin.
- *Literal grounding rule:* grounding a figure only by "within ±$1 / ±0.5 pts of any summary figure" — no rounding allowance and no check that the same item is meant — gives 48.1% grounded, 20.5% derived and 28.6% unsupported.

## Hand check

*This section and the next were written by hand after the run; everything else on this page is generated by the AI Eval view.*

75 figures were drawn at random (seed `20261025`, 25 per class) and checked by hand against the summary the model received:

| Class | Checked | Verdict |
|---|---:|---|
| Grounded | 25 | **25 correct.** Each is a summary figure, used for the item it stands for. |
| Derived ✓ | 25 | **25 correct values.** 1 was credited through a coincidental rule: a "49% savings rate in both months" matched *top 7 categories ÷ income* (49.3%) instead of the monthly rates. |
| Unsupported | 25 | **21 not supported by the data:** advice, benchmarks, assumed APRs, monthly figures built on a guessed period length. **4 correct derivations the matcher can't verify:** "29% more than Spotify", "3 months of expenses ≈ $7,500", and "about $1,000" twice (actual $1,051–$1,067, more than the 2.5% rounding allowed). |

The matcher errs on the conservative side. It accepted no wrong figure in the sample, while about 1 in 6 unsupported figures (4 of 25, a small sample) is really a correct derivation. So the traceable share above is more likely low than high. The chance-match rate is a stress test of what the matcher *could* accept, not what it did.

**What the 200 unsupported figures are** (keyword heuristic): 139 advice or targets, 29 benchmarks, 21 assumptions, 11 claims about the data. Of those 11:
- 4 are correct arithmetic spanning sentences;
- 2 are "about $1,000" for $1,051–$1,067;
- 1 is budget advice the heuristic missed;
- 4 are monthly figures built on a guessed period length (see below).

### Findings about the AI feature itself

- **Guessed period length.** The summary says `Period: All time` with no dates, so the model has to guess how many months the totals cover.
  - One answer assumed about 6.6 months (groceries ~$470/month, shopping ~$280, fees ~$45).
  - Another assumed 6 (groceries ~$516/month, "10.5% of income").
  - The data covers 5.7 months, so groceries are actually about $545/month.
  - Adding the date span to `buildFinancialSummary()` would remove this kind of error.
- **Truncated answers.** 15 of 24 answers hit `max_tokens` (1,500), despite the system prompt asking for under 500 words. The AI tab shows them cut off mid-sentence with no notice. Figures in the cut-off text aren't counted here.

## Changes made after the run

The run was first scored in the browser. Reading the real answers exposed three bugs in the matcher, which were fixed before this page was generated from the same raw answers (`eval-results.json`):

1. **Table cells were checked without their column header**, so a `Sep` / `Oct` column didn't count as naming the month. This moved 3 figures from unsupported to derived.
2. **A percentage could "restate" a different one within ±0.5 pts.** For example, "-0.4 pts" matched "-0.1%". Restatements now have to match within the precision either figure is written with, the same rule derived percentages already followed. No counts changed; one figure is now credited for the right reason.
3. **Advice and benchmark tags missed common phrasing** ("capping", "trimming", "keep", "saves") and table columns headed "Benchmark". This affects tags only.

**Before the fixes:** 48.6% grounded, 19.8% derived, 28.9% unsupported. **After:** 48.6%, 20.2%, 28.4%. No rule was added for the correct derivations the matcher misses (listed above), so the score wasn't tuned to these answers.

## Method

1. **Data.** The eval view loads the three CSVs in `samples/` (refusing any file whose SHA-256 differs from the committed one) and parses them the way an import does — `detectSchema()`, `normalizeRow()`, occurrence suffixes, `detectTransfers()` — inside a sandboxed copy of `State` with no overrides, rules, manual balances, budgets or debts, and the date range set to All Time. The real `State` is restored before any request is sent; nothing is persisted.
2. **Requests.** `buildFinancialSummary()` produces the summary below once; each of the 8 `AI_PROMPTS` is sent 3 times through `callClaude()`, exactly as the AI Analysis tab sends it (same system prompt, model and `max_tokens`).
3. **Extraction.** Every dollar amount (`$1,234.56`, `-$617`, `$4.5K`, the second half of `$200–300`) and percentage (`48.4%`, `12 percent`, `3 percentage points`, both ends of `10–15%`) in each answer is one figure; repeated mentions count each time. Plain numbers without `$` or `%` are not counted.
4. **Classification**, in this order:
   - **Grounded** — equals a figure in the summary within ±$1 / ±0.5 pts. To keep coincidences out, the item it stands for must be named nearby (e.g. "income" and "September") unless the match is exact to the cent / 0.1 pt, or is a dollar amount of $100 or more. A rounded figure ("$5K", "48%") may match within the rounding its precision implies — half a unit of its last digit, at most 2.5% of the value — but only when it is named nearby.
   - **Derived ✓** — arithmetic on summary figures, verified in code: an explicit equation in the answer (`$2,057 × 12 = $24,684`) whose result checks out and that uses at least one figure already grounded or derived; arithmetic on such figures on the same line (sums, differences, ratios, a stated % of an amount, ×/÷ a stated number of months), never resting on the figure being checked; or one of 107 standard derivations of this summary (period net, category shares of spending or income, monthly savings rates, month-over-month changes, 2- and 3-month averages, ×12 annualisation, net worth + 12 × average net, subscription totals). A derivation tied to a category or month only counts when that category or month is named nearby. Tolerance for dollar amounts: the larger of $1, 0.25% of the value, or the figure's rounding; for percentages: the figure's rounding (±0.05 pts for "49.3%", ±0.5 for "49%"), at least ±0.15 pts. A figure that repeats an earlier verified one counts as that one.
   - **Math error** — an explicit equation whose arithmetic doesn't hold.
   - **Forecast (unverified)** — only in "12-month net worth prediction": projections resting on assumptions the code can't verify. By the eval's rules they are treated as derived, not unsupported, but they are reported separately and not counted as verified.
   - **Unsupported** — everything else. Each one is tagged with a keyword heuristic as *advice/target* (a recommended budget or goal), *benchmark* (a rule of thumb), *assumption*, or *claim about the data*.

## Limitations

- Grounding is not correctness: a figure can match the data and still be used wrongly (e.g. a period total described as monthly). The eval checks where numbers come from, not whether the advice is good.
- The summary shows "Period: All time" without dates, so the model doesn't know the period is ~6 months; per-month figures it infers from period totals usually land in *unsupported*.
- Derivations outside the checked forms (multi-step reasoning across sentences, rounding chains) are counted as unsupported — the derived share is a lower bound in that sense, while the chance-match rate above bounds how much the matcher over-accepts.
- The *advice / benchmark / assumption / claim* reading of unsupported figures is a keyword heuristic.
- One dataset, one model, 24 answers. Responses vary run to run (default temperature); see the repeat table.

## Reproduce

1. Save an Anthropic API key in **Settings → General**.
2. Open DevTools and run `showView('ai-eval')`. (Over `file://`, pick the three files in `samples/` when asked.)
3. Click **Run eval** (24 calls). When it finishes, **Download EVAL.md** and **Download results JSON** (raw answers and every figure's classification; **Load results JSON** re-scores a saved run without new calls).

Raw answers and per-figure classifications for this run: [`docs/eval-runs/v1.10.0/eval-results.json`](eval-results.json).
